I’m Isha Kaushik, a researcher and builder mapping the market forces behind human health and wellness. Currently stress-testing hypergrowth models for the longevity market.

Isha Kaushik

I’m half North, half South Indian, so I get roasted by both sides and fed by both sides, which balances things out. Balance is also how I work: the research tells me where the bottlenecks are, and the build side is where I do something about them, designing an operating system that helps heavy health science scale fast, and legally. I grew up wanting to be a clinician, then an inventor of therapies, and eventually realized I’d rather spend a lifetime improving how a breakthrough travels from the lab to the people who need it. Happily losing entire weekends to it. Deets in the workshop, and so am I.

Can’t leave a health idea alone until I’ve tinkered with it. Some of them are public, like an early PTSD protocol (REM-sleep markers), VICE (food delivery conversion), a BCI R&D (neuromarketing), and a nonprofit rebrand (for next gen farmers). My first two solo hackathon builds, JudgementOS (clinical reasoning) and ArcusOS (AI-native school), earned honorable mentions by clinicians and educators at NYEdtech Hackathon.

I recently earned my Masters in Neuroscience from Columbia University (’26), as the youngest scholar admitted to the program, with minors in Education and Design Engineering. My thesis evaluated DL architectures for BCI thought-to-text, backed by a field audit and an early governance framework. Before that, I completed my Bachelors in Neuroscience at Amity University, Noida (’24), as a merit scholar and silver medalist.

Biology only got me so far, so I went and found the rest myself. I studied law and ethics with Prof. Adriel Brown at Stanford Medicine and through independent immersion at Columbia Law, then went deep on philosophy and education at Teachers College, where my supervised research became a paper linking aesthetics, epistemic justice, and scientific curiosity.

Most of what I know comes from people, usually by proving what I didn’t know. Some questions are best explored outside the classroom and labs, and here are some field notes from the people, places, and ideas that have influenced the direction of my efforts.
