Isha Kaushik

I'm Isha Kaushik, a researcher and builder working on the science, systems, and business of human health. Raised in Noida, India, with roots in both the north and south. And so I am constitutionally incapable of taking sides.

That comes in handy in my research, which looks at the ethics and regulation of health innovation, where clinicians, founders, regulators and patients are all partly right. I grew up wanting to be a clinician, then wanted to invent the therapies, then realized I could spend a lifetime on either, or build the infrastructure that delivers every breakthrough to the people waiting for it. PsyMed Ventures' Neuro Founders Program in 2025 pushed me to pair the research with building in commercialization. That became the company I'm building now, a venture in commercial health and wellness, with the research division inside it. Launching soon.

Along the way, I kept testing ideas by building them. It started with PTSD early detection, clinical sleep research on REM-based early warning, then the VICE Framework on how food shapes design decisions, then a validation framework for a neuromarketing BCI. Later came a nonprofit rebrand that gave a food-systems brand its story back, and two solo hackathon builds, a clinical reasoning tool and ArcusOS, an AI-native school model (honorable mention).

My master's in neuroscience at Columbia (2026) focused on cognitive and computational neuroscience, and I was the youngest scholar admitted to the program. The thesis, 66 pages, evaluated deep learning architectures for BCI thought-to-text, with a field audit and a preliminary governance framework. Clinical ML research at the Zuckerman Institute came before stepping out to build, and the Founders' and Michael Eisner Awards were a kind recognition along the way. It all started with a neuroscience major at Amity University, Noida, with minors in medical physiology and psychology, graduating in 2024 as a merit scholar and silver medalist, with awards for cross-disciplinary research and teaching. Connectionist modelling of neural networks is still my favorite conversation starter, and occasionally my conversation ender.

Health systems are shaped by far more than biology, so the rest of my training came from unusual angles.

Wherever the truth about health systems seemed to be hiding, I was happy to go knocking. I studied law, ethics and society under Professor Adriel Brown of Stanford, through Columbia Law School's Law and Philosophy Programming and the Kernochan Center for Media and Arts (2025–26), and wrote a philosophy paper at Teachers College on aesthetics, epistemic justice, and how fascination shapes scientific, inventive and creative pursuits, alongside training in education and human development. In Spring 2025, design engineering through TC's MST department and the Design for Social Innovation cohort at SIPA × Columbia Entrepreneurship Design Studio taught me to build for the people living inside these systems, and I made all the classic first-timer mistakes while delivering rebranding growth results. Rules, questions, people: each angle got me a little closer.

Most of what I've learned came from people, so a good part of my time went to them.

A Deep Tech Fellowship at AUUP (2022) meant sitting with 20+ public health officers and 50+ chronic-disease patients across India, and a funded place in BiteLab's Healthtech Fellowship followed, which I stepped away from to spend time in remote communities instead. As VP of NeurotechX Columbia (2025–26), we put five schools in one room for a hackathon and started a podcast to keep the conversation going, and I moderated Women in Neuroscience debates on gender equity, with 200+ in the room. I also passed it on, with a talk at MaSC Lab, TC on attention economy and neural rewiring, and by mentoring a cohort of 20 life-science undergrads at Project EduAccess, which showed me which of my own ideas were still half-formed.

Writing lives on Substack and sometimes Medium. Off the clock: trained Hindustani classical vocalist and instrumentalist (Sangeet Prabhakar, 2021), Mandarin learner (chopsticks still in progress), and collector of material for a future memoir. I'm a selective reader: I love books, but only nonfiction (currently How to Save the World in 6 (Not So Easy) Steps). Fiction I'd rather watch, which is why Black Mirror is on.

Hard problems where health, technology and regulation meet, and the teams working on them, are what I'm after. Say hi, or send pop culture references.
