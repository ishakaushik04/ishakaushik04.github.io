Isha Kaushik

Hi, I'm Isha, a researcher and builder working on the science, systems, and business of human health. Raised in Noida, a half North and half South Indian background leaves me constitutionally incapable of taking sides.

That comes in handy in my research on the ethics and regulation of health innovation, where clinicians, founders, regulators and patients are all partly right. I grew up imagining myself as a clinician, then an inventor of therapies, but realized midway I could spend a lifetime on either or build the infrastructure that delivers every breakthrough to the people waiting for it.
That's what I am building a venture in with research built in.

Along the way I kept tinkering with different ideas in health and wellness: an early PTSD assessment framework using REM-sleep markers; VICE, a conversion optimization for Indian food delivery; a validation framework for a neuromarketing BCI; and a nonprofit rebrand that made its exclusivity feel open to a new generation of farmers. My first two solo hackathon projects: JudgementOS, a clinical reasoning tool, and ArcusOS, an AI-native school model earned honorable mentions at NYEdtech Hackathon.

Recently earned my Masters in Neuroscience from Columbia University (Class of '26), with minors in Education and Design Engineering. Was the youngest scholar admitted to the program having granted Founders and Michael Eisner Awards. My thesis evaluated deep learning architectures for BCI thought-to-text, with a field audit and a preliminary governance framework. Briefly conducted clinical ML research at the Zuckerman Institute before stepping out to build. Prior to this, I completed my Bachelors in Neuroscience from Amity University, Noida, with minors in Medical Physiology and Psychology (Class of '24). Graduated as a merit scholar and silver medalist, with awards for cross-disciplinary research and teaching. Connectionist Modelling of neural networks still keeps me awake some nights.

Realized quite early in life that health systems are shaped by far more than biology, so I went knocking from other angles. Studied law, ethics and society with Professor Adriel Brown of Stanford Medicine, alongside supervised seminars and independent studies through Columbia Law School's Law and Philosophy Programming and the Kernochan Center for Media and Arts (2025-26). At Teachers College, part of my graduate coursework in philosophy and education culminated in a philosophy paper on aesthetics, epistemic justice, and scientific curiosity. Design engineering at TC's MST department and social innovation at SIPA × Columbia Entrepreneurship Design Studio taught me that the answer to organic growth is ridiculous volume of precision tests.

Most of what I practice comes from people: a Deep Tech Fellowship in 2022 took me to 20+ public health officers and 50+ chronic-disease patients. I later stepped away from a funded Healthtech Fellowship in Boston to spend time in remote communities instead. Had fun leading NeurotechX Columbia on partnerships for hackathons and podcasts and moderating gender-equity debates with 200+ people at WiN. Also guest lectured at TC's MaSC Lab about the attention economy and neural rewiring, then mentored dozens of life-science undergrads at Project EduAccess, who helped me see where my own thinking was still unfinished.

Off the clock, I write on Substack and sometimes Medium. I'm a trained Hindustani classical vocalist and instrumentalist (Sangeet Prabhakar, 2021), learning Mandarin (chopsticks remain a separate challenge), and regularly engage in dumb stuff to study the consequences firsthand. I love books, but usually just non-fiction; currently reading How to Save the World in 6 (Not So Easy) Steps. Fiction I'd rather watch, which is why Black Mirror is on.

The best conversations I ever had all started with pop culture references. So feel free to send some my way!
