Hi, I’m Isha, a researcher and builder working on the science, systems, and business of human health. Currently designing and testing hypergrowth market models for health education at scale.

Isha Kaushik

I design the AI systems that make heavy health science scale fast, legally. I’m half North, half South Indian, so I get roasted by both sides and fed by both sides, which balances it out. I grew up wanting to be a clinician, then to invent therapies. Realized along the way, I would rather spent a lifetime improving what gets a breakthrough from the lab to those who need it. Happily losing entire weekends down for it. Deets in the workshop, and so am I.

Along the way I kept tinkering with different ideas in health and wellness: an early PTSD assessment protocol using REM-sleep markers; VICE, a conversion optimization for Indian food delivery; a validation framework for a neuromarketing BCI; and a nonprofit rebrand that made its exclusivity feel open to a new generation of farmers. My first two solo hackathon projects, JudgementOS (a clinical reasoning tool) and ArcusOS (an AI-native school model), earned honorable mentions at NYEdtech Hackathon.

Recently earned my Masters in Neuroscience from Columbia University ('26), with minors in Education and Design Engineering. Was the youngest scholar admitted to the program, having been granted Founders and Michael Eisner Awards. My thesis evaluated deep learning architectures for BCI thought-to-text, with a field audit and a preliminary governance framework. Briefly conducted clinical ML research at the Zuckerman Institute before stepping out to build. Previously, I completed my Bachelors in Neuroscience from Amity University, Noida ('24) with minors in Medical Physiology and Psychology. Graduated as a merit scholar and silver medalist, with awards for cross-disciplinary research and teaching. Connectionist modeling of neural networks still keeps me awake some nights.

Biology only got me so far on health, so I went and found the rest myself. I studied law and ethics with Prof. Adriel Brown at Stanford Medicine and through independent immersion at Columbia Law, then went deep on philosophy and education at Teachers College, where my supervised research produced a paper on aesthetics, epistemic justice, and scientific curiosity. Design engineering under Columbia Entrepreneurship and SIPA taught me one thing: good ideas need a ridiculous number of precision tests.

Most of what I know comes from people, usually by proving what I didn't know. Some questions are best explored outside the classroom and labs. These are a few of my field notes from the people, places, and ideas that have influenced the direction of my efforts.

Off the clock, I write on Substack and sometimes Medium. I'm a trained Hindustani classical vocalist and instrumentalist (Sangeet Prabhakar, 2021), learning Mandarin (chopsticks remain a separate challenge), and regularly engage in dumb stuff to study the consequences firsthand. I love books, but usually just non-fiction; currently reading How to Save the World in 6 (Not So Easy) Steps. Fiction I'd rather watch, which is why Black Mirror is on.

The best conversations I ever had all started with pop culture references. So feel free to send some my way!
