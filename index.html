the playbook

I grew up meaning to be a clinician, then an inventor of therapies, until I saw how painful the road still is from a breakthrough in the lab to the people who need it. I’d rather spend a lifetime improving that journey, and now lose entire weekends to it, happily. I’m half North and half South Indian, so I grew up on a fun mix of everything, and straight-up paths never worked for me. I love simple things, I just have trouble doing them simply. I tried deep research and loved its core, but not the politics. I tried industry and loved the relative speed and scale, but missed the rigor and the philosophy. So I’m reinventing the playbook for myself: I lead with research, then scale it through real-world infrastructure to get the most out of whatever I contribute.

the receipts

Recently earned my Master’s in Neuroscience from Columbia University (’26), as the youngest scholar admitted to the program, with a focus on combining Education and Design Engineering. My thesis evaluated deep learning architectures for BCI thought-to-text, backed by a field audit and an early governance framework. Before that, I completed my Bachelor’s in Neuroscience at Amity University, Noida (’24), minors in Medical Physiology and Psychology as a merit scholar and silver medalist.

the workshop

Tinkered with ideas that deal with how people heal, act, or learn; public ones include an early PTSD protocol (REM-sleep markers), VICE (food delivery conversion), a BCI R&D project (neuromarketing), and a nonprofit rebrand (for next-gen farmers). My first two solo hackathon builds, JudgementOS (clinical reasoning) and ArcusOS (an AI-native school), earned honorable mentions from clinicians and educators at the NYEdtech Hackathon.

the guardrails

Blame Jurassic Park here. I went in a science fan and came out convinced that brilliant biology without law, ethics, and judgment ends with someone being chased. My friends fed the habit, gifting me the novels one birthday at a time, and I still guard them. Ian Malcolm was right and so I went and found the rest myself: studied law and ethics with Prof. Adriel Brown at Stanford Medicine and through independent immersion at Columbia Law School, then went deep on philosophy and education at Teachers College, where my supervised research became a paper linking aesthetics, epistemic justice, and scientific curiosity.

the people part

Everything that runs my backend, I borrowed from people kind enough to correct me. I learned in research labs, design studios, and committee rooms, then started handing it back: a guest lecture @MaSc Lab on the attention economy, mentoring student pods from the Global South in foundation and ethics of bioscience research @Project EduAccess, and previously moderated debates on gender equity in science @WiN . The best teachers were where data couldn't reach as far as I checked; gave up a funded healthtech fellowship in Boston to sit with them and hear what a chronic condition does to an ordinary day.
