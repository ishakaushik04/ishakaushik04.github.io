Hi, I’m Isha Kaushik. I research the incentives, evidence, and capital that decide which health and wellness markets endure. Currently building the hypergrowth models longevity businesses will run on.

Isha Kaushik

I’m half North and half South Indian; roasted by both sides, fed by both too, so the books balance. They (roughly) balance at work too. The research tells me where the bottleneck is, and the build side is where I deal with it, designing an operating system that helps heavy health science scale fast, without outrunning its evidence.

I grew up meaning to be a clinician, then an inventor of therapies. Then I witnessed how painful the road still is from a breakthrough in the lab to the people who need it. I’d rather spend a lifetime improving that journey, and now lose entire weekends to it, happily.

Background

I recently earned my Master’s in Neuroscience from Columbia University (’26), as the youngest scholar admitted to the program, with a focus on combining Education and Design Engineering. My thesis evaluated deep learning architectures for BCI thought-to-text, backed by a field audit and an early governance framework. Before that, I completed my Bachelor’s in Neuroscience at Amity University, Noida (’24), minors in Medical Physiology and Psychology as a merit scholar and silver medalist.

Along the way, I couldn’t leave an idea alone until I’d tinkered with it, especially one about how people heal, choose, or learn. Some of the results are public: an early PTSD protocol (REM-sleep markers), VICE (food delivery conversion), a BCI R&D project (neuromarketing), and a nonprofit rebrand (for next-gen farmers). My first two solo hackathon builds, JudgementOS (clinical reasoning) and ArcusOS (an AI-native school), earned honorable mentions from clinicians and educators at the NYEdtech Hackathon.

Blame Jurassic Park here. I went in a science fan and came out convinced that brilliant biology without law, ethics, and judgment ends with someone being chased. My friends fed the habit, gifting me the novels one birthday at a time, and I still guard them. Ian Malcolm was right and so I went and found the rest myself: studied law and ethics with Prof. Adriel Brown at Stanford Medicine and through independent immersion at Columbia Law, then went deep on philosophy and education at Teachers College, where my supervised research became a paper linking aesthetics, epistemic justice, and scientific curiosity.

Most of what I know comes from people, usually by proving what I didn’t know. I learned in research labs, design studios, and committee rooms, then started handing it back: a guest lecture in a senior Columbia lab, mentoring students from the Global South towards higher education, and moderating global debates on gender equity in science. The best teachers were where data couldn't reach as far as I checked; gave up a funded healthtech fellowship in Boston to sit with them and hear what a chronic condition does to an ordinary day.
