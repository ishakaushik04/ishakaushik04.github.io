VICE: A Weighted Framework for Behavioral Drivers of Conversion in Indian Food Delivery

Isha Kaushik

Research Assistant, Cross-Modal Perception Lab, University of Oxford

Guided by Prof. Charles Spence and Tianyi Zhang · Evidence base: 40+ peer-reviewed studies

In short

A four-part framework (Visual, Informational, Cultural, Experiential) that turns food-psychology research into testable changes to Indian food-delivery listings. Built from 40+ studies and not yet tested.

AbstractGastrophysics has decades of evidence on how sensory cues shape food choice, but little of it has been translated to Indian digital ordering, where empirical studies are close to absent. Forty-plus peer-reviewed studies were synthesized into VICE, four pillars (Visual 35%, Informational 25%, Cultural 20%, Experiential 20%) weighted from effect sizes across studies and adjusted for Indian market traits. The pillars are assumed to interact multiplicatively. The framework is complete and untested. It was written so that a platform product manager can launch A/B tests the following week.

Background

India’s food-delivery market is projected to reach $28B by 2030. Platforms optimize delivery speed and discounts, and interface-level behavioral science is little used. The relevant literature is Western, and empirical studies of Indian food-delivery behavior are close to nil. Each recalibration in Table 1 is therefore an explicit assumption. Festival and seasonal food traditions add a further layer.

Method

  1. Literature. A targeted read, not a systematic review, for what could work in a digital interface: gastrophysics and cross-modal perception, digital food commerce, cultural food psychology, hedonic versus utilitarian motivation, and Indian market studies (sparse) (Fig. 1).
  2. Convergence. Four behavioral dimensions recurred as conversion drivers.
  3. Weighting. Weights derived from effect sizes across studies, then adjusted for Indian market traits.
  4. Operationalization. Each pillar converted into tactics that a platform can A/B test.

Framework

VICE has four pillars, weighted as in Fig. 2.

Visual

Online, the dish photograph is the only sensory input before purchase and must stand in for aroma, sound and social cues. Professional food styling raised perceived taste quality by 23% (Int. J. Hospitality Management, 2022).

TacticsStyled high-resolution photography; menu layout for F-pattern mobile scanning; close-ups, portion size and action shots; color matched to cuisine.

PlatformBronze, Silver and Gold photo tiers for restaurants, tied to conversion tracking.

Informational

Indian users research before ordering (high uncertainty avoidance). Structured detail shortens the decision.

TacticsRatings with counts, “Most Ordered”, dated and photo reviews; prep time, live restaurant status, sourcing; “home-style” versus “restaurant-style” tags; “pairs well with…”; layered detail (name and price first, then description, then nutrition and allergens).

PlatformA Trust Score from photo quality, review recency, menu completeness and response rate. Higher scores earn better placement.

Cultural

India has 28+ food cultures. Platforms that contextualize rather than standardize should gain share.

TacticsFestival triggers (Diwali feasts, Ramadan iftar timing, monsoon chai); family orders (“serves 4”) versus individual orders (speed, portion); price framing for value seekers versus aspirational buyers; localization beyond translation (“ghar jaisa”).

PlatformRegional flavor profiles from order history and location. A Kolkata user searching “fish curry” sees Bengali-style options first.

Experiential

Description language shapes expectation before the first bite. Pleasure framing moves an order from satisfying hunger to treating oneself, which raises willingness to pay. Over-description backfires by sounding too good to be true.

TacticsIndulgent framing on weekends and practical framing at weekday lunch; words that match the photo (crispy, sizzling, velvety); time-of-day framing; nostalgia (“Mom’s recipe style”).

PlatformA sensory-language library in English, Hindi and regional languages, A/B tested into a proprietary database.

Interaction

The weights do not sum to a conversion rate. Each pillar is assumed to amplify the others, so effects compound rather than add. This premise is untested. Fig. 3 renders an illustrative listing with each pillar switched on or off. Table 2 gives the text of the two end states.

Contributions

LedIdentified the gap and scoped it as applied synthesis rather than literature review; mined 40+ studies for conversion-relevant findings; designed the four pillars and the 35/25/20/20 weights; adapted Western findings to India with assumptions stated; wrote platform-executable tactics.

LabDirection to the relevant literature (visual-taste interactions, hedonic motivation, interface psychology); the gastrophysics foundation; critical feedback on the logic, the weights, and which findings can support applied claims.

Discussion

Platforms rarely apply interface-level behavioral science. For researchers, VICE narrows the gap between gastrophysics in the lab and its use in market, with cultural grounding. For restaurants, as platforms consolidate power, scoring well on VICE-type criteria becomes a condition of visibility.

Limitations

VICE rests on Western research adapted without Indian validation data. The weights are provisional and the interaction premise is untested. Next steps: a pilot with one or two Indian platforms, then extension to cloud kitchens, grocery delivery and reservations. Real conversion data will refine the weights.
