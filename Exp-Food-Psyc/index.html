The gap

Food delivery apps run on generic e-commerce rules.

Oxford's lab has decades of research on how sensory cues change what we choose and enjoy. No one had translated it for Indian digital ordering. I proposed building that bridge. The lab pointed me to the literature, and I built the implementation model.

How I built it

Four steps, from papers to tactics.

  1. Mine the literature

    Not a systematic review. A targeted read for what could work in a digital interface.

    • Gastrophysics & cross-modal perception
    • Digital food commerce
    • Cultural food psychology
    • Hedonic vs utilitarian motivation
    • Indian market studies (sparse, which became a finding)

  2. Find what converges

    Four behavioral dimensions kept showing up as conversion drivers. They behave multiplicatively: each pillar amplifies the others.

  3. Weight and adapt

    Weights come from effect sizes across studies, then adjusted for Indian market traits.

  4. Turn it into tactics

    The test: a platform product manager reads VICE and launches A/B tests the next week.

The framework

VICE: four weighted pillars.

V 35%I 25%C 20%E 20%

Visual design · 35%

The plate you see determines the taste you expect

Online, the dish photo is the only sensory input before purchase. It has to stand in for aroma, sound and social cues.

  • Styled, high-res photography
  • Color by cuisine: warm reds for spice, greens for fresh, golden-brown for richness
  • Menu layout for F-pattern mobile scanning
  • Close-ups, portion size, action shots

Professional food styling raised perceived taste quality by 23% (Int. J. Hospitality Management, 2022).

For platforms: Bronze, Silver and Gold photo tiers for restaurants, tied to conversion tracking.

Informational architecture · 25%

Uncertainty kills conversion; transparency builds trust

Indian users research before they order (high uncertainty avoidance). Well-structured detail speeds the decision up.

  • Layered detail: name and price first, tap for description, then nutrition and allergens
  • Ratings with counts, “Most Ordered”, dated and photo reviews
  • Prep time, live restaurant status, sourcing
  • “Home-style” vs “restaurant-style” tags
  • “Pairs well with…”

For platforms: a Trust Score from photo quality, review recency, menu completeness and response rate. Higher score earns better placement.

Cultural contextualization · 20%

One India doesn't exist in food psychology

India has 28+ food cultures. Platforms that contextualize instead of standardizing win more share.

NorthRichness, spice, breads
SouthRice base, sambhar and chutney variety, filter coffee
EastFish and seafood, mustard oil
WestChaat and snack culture, sweet-savory balance
  • Festival triggers: Diwali feasts, Ramadan iftar timing, monsoon chai
  • Family orders (“serves 4”) vs individual orders (speed, portion)
  • Price framing for value seekers vs aspirational buyers
  • Localization beyond translation: “ghar jaisa”

For platforms: regional flavor profiles from order history and location. A Kolkata user searching fish curry sees Bengali-style options first.

Experiential cues · 20%

Language shapes taste before the first bite

How a dish is described changes how it's experienced. Pleasure framing moves people from satisfying hunger to treating themselves, which raises willingness to pay.

Poor“Chicken curry with rice”
Optimized“Tender chicken in aromatic, slow-simmered curry with fragrant basmati rice”
  • Indulgent framing on weekends, practical on weekday lunch
  • Words that match the photo: crispy, sizzling, velvety
  • Time-of-day framing, morning to midnight
  • Nostalgia: “Mom's recipe style”

Over-describing backfires. It starts to sound too good to be true.

For platforms: a sensory-language library in English, Hindi and regional languages, A/B tested into a proprietary database.

Rollout

How a platform would use it.

  1. Audit

    Score the top 100 restaurants on VICE. Check whether scores track actual order rates.

  2. Pilot

    Pick 20 restaurants. A/B test VICE-optimized listings against control. Measure conversion lift, basket size, repeat orders.

  3. Enable partners

    Toolkit of photo guidelines, description templates and cultural checklists. Better placement and lower commission for high scorers.

  4. Build into ranking

    Feed VICE scores into the algorithm. Personalize weights per user, since some are visual-driven and others information-driven.

Why it matters

Three audiences.

Platforms

Most optimization targets delivery speed and discounts. Interface-level behavioral science is barely used.

Researchers

It narrows the lab-to-market gap for gastrophysics, with cultural grounding.

Restaurants

As platforms consolidate power, knowing how to score well on VICE becomes a survival skill.

  • Designed the four pillars and the 35/25/20/20 weights

What the lab gave

Limits and next

Built on Western research, adapted without Indian validation data. Real conversion numbers will refine the weights.

Next: pilot with one or two Indian platforms, then extend to cloud kitchens, grocery delivery and reservations. The best frameworks aren't comprehensive. They're useful.
