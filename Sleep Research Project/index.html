Project Title

A short 2-3 line brief describing the project

Introduction

Research Domain: Clinical Neuroscience

The Problem: Unaddressed REM sleep loss in PTSD represents a missed opportunity for targeted, cost-effective interventions rooted in precise neurochemical insights.

Approach / Journey

Phase 1: From late-May to early-August of 2022, I conducted a preliminary study on dimensional approaches to measure sleep characteristics. Guided by Dr. Vijay Kumar at AINN, I critically read 40+ scientific articles and reports on links between sleep disturbances and PTSD and prepared a 10-page analysis report for categorical sleep study measures – including subjective and objective classes. The report access can be requested here.

Phase 2: From April till August 2023, I (along with my co-author Harshpreet Kaur) conducted a rigorous review to recapitulate the available evidence linking sleep disturbances in PTSD in a complex multidirectional relationship. This project was guided by Prof. Birendra Nath Mallick, a world-renowned sleep neuroscientist, infamous for his work on REMS.

In this project, I co-authored a review exploring the neurobiological links between PTSD and REM sleep disturbances, with a specific focus on noradrenaline's role. We investigated how disrupted REM sleep - common in PTSD - interacts with elevated noradrenaline levels, contributing to the condition's persistence and symptom severity. The paper also evaluated emerging noradrenaline-targeting treatments aimed at restoring REM sleep and mitigating PTSD symptoms. This work deepened our understanding of how sleep physiology and neurochemistry intertwine in trauma-related disorders.

Solutions & Key Takeaways

This work contributes to the development of new assessment protocols that are currently being tested and implemented in several trauma centres across India. It provides insight into how sleep-focused interventions can complement PTSD treatments and informs the design of targeted therapies that address REM sleep disruption and neurochemical imbalances.
