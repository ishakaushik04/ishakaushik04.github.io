The problem

How do you validate a device that claims to read minds?

Global ad spend was projected to cross $1 trillion in 2025, yet brands still judge creative by what people say they noticed and felt. People rationalize, forget and reshape experiences. A BCI can skip that, but nothing exists to check the BCI against.

Self-report

What people say. Easy to collect, unreliable by nature.

Direct neural measure

What the brain does. Promising, but attention, emotion and memory are subjective states, so there's no gold-standard scan to compare with.

5cognitive domains validated at once
45 minper session, using real ads
0gold-standard scans to check against

The idea

Triangulate the truth.

Existing paradigms test attention or emotion or memory, one at a time. This framework tests five domains together. If neural signals line up with independent behavioral measures across several constructs, confidence in the device compounds.

Neural signal⇄Recall+Self-report ratings+Behavior→Convergence = provisional ground truth

Four tests it had to pass

Convergent

Do neural measures track independent behavioral ones? “High attention” with poor recall is a failure.

Discriminant

Can it tell ads apart? Calling every ad “moderately engaging” is useless.

Reliable

Consistent within a session, across sessions, and between analysts.

Sensitive

Detects differences big enough for brands to act on: recall, preference, purchase intent.

The domains are causally linked, and testing those links is where the validation power came from.

The setup

Real ads, real conditions.

Real ads are messy, and that's the point. If the device only works on clean lab stimuli, it isn't commercially viable. Everything ran on Indian ads with Indian participants, because emotional triggers and narrative conventions differ from the Western norms most neuromarketing is built on.

Typical lab studyThis validation
StimuliSimple, researcher-madeReal ads brands paid to air
Scale100+ trials per condition45-minute sessions
ControlStrictMusic, length, celebrities and production quality all vary
AudienceMostly WesternIndian participants, Indian baselines

What broke, and the fix

Four problems.

1 · The ground truth problem

ProblemNo objective right answer for a mental state.

FixConvergent proxies. When behavior, self-report and neural patterns agree, treat it as provisional ground truth.

2 · Dry-EEG noise

ProblemDry electrodes are practical but noisier than lab-grade wet EEG. Movement, interference and contact add error.

FixThresholds for acceptable signal-to-noise, artifact rejection rules, and statistics that carry the uncertainty.

3 · Real ads confound everything

ProblemWas it memorable for the music, the celebrity, the humor or the product?

FixMany ads across categories, within-subject comparisons, and relative questions (which ad is more memorable?) over absolute scores.

4 · Rigor vs commercial speed

ProblemAcademic validation takes hundreds of participants and months. Commercial timelines don't.

FixPrioritize attention, memory and emotion. Maximize information per session. Not complete by academic standards, but enough for commercial confidence, and built to improve with more data.

What I led, under senior guidance

  • Literature synthesis across neuroscience, psychology and neuromarketing
  • Ground-truth logic: convergent measures where no single one is definitive
  • Scientific backing for each of the five domains, within dry-EEG limits
  • The 45-minute protocol: flow, stimulus presentation, measurement order

What I co-developed with the team

  • Turning “high frontal alpha” into “this ad will be remembered”
  • Trading off experimental control against real-world validity
  • Indian adaptation: regional language processing, emotion display rules, family and festival messaging, celebrity endorsements

Outcome

It shipped, and it travels.

  • Helped the startup take its dry-EEG device to market with scientific backing. It is positioned as one of India's leading BCIs for cognitive measurement.
  • The company later moved into cognitive rehabilitation. The validation work still underpins its product claims.
  • The method can adapt to other BCIs, other metrics and other cultures, and shows validation can work outside a perfect lab.

Limits: this was pre-market. The real test is whether predictions in client projects match downstream outcomes like sales, awareness and preference. Validation beyond India is also open.

What it changed for me

From “can we measure this?” to “should we?”

Who owns brain data? How do we stop cognitive surveillance? Those questions became my master's work in neuroethics, including a thesis on BCI thought-to-text with a governance framework.
