Scaling Sustainable Agriculture Through Strategic Brand Repositioning

Design Research Lead | Columbia Entrepreneurship Design Studio × SIPA

Led qualitative synthesis and audience perception mapping for a women-led agtech nonprofit, translating 30+ stakeholder interviews into actionable rebrand strategy. Delivered dual-audience positioning framework that drove 40% click-through increase on demo day, creating a scalable blueprint for expanding apprentice pipelines and donor engagement.

The Context: A Systems-Level Agricultural Crisis

The U.S. faces a farmer demographic cliff—average age 58, only 6% under 35—threatening food security and sustainable agriculture infrastructure. Amber Waves, operating at the intersection of agriculture and education, runs an intensive 8-month, 2,000-hour apprenticeship training the next generation of regenerative farmers. But a critical misperception problem was throttling their growth: prospective apprentices and donors alike saw them as an exclusive East Hampton for-profit farm, not the nonprofit training hub producing 20+ sustainable agriculture leaders annually.

This wasn't just a branding challenge—it was a market access barrier preventing talent acquisition and capital formation for a mission-critical training pipeline. In an industry where farmer shortages directly correlate with nutrition outcomes, food system resilience, and rural economic vitality, perception misalignment creates downstream public health and economic consequences.

The Process: From Data Chaos to Strategic Clarity

Discovery & Initial Hypothesis

Our five-person interdisciplinary team—spanning engineering, education, social work, and medicine—conducted rapid stakeholder immersion. I led qualitative data synthesis, processing interviews with founders, apprentices, farm crew, and community partners into 10+ detailed analysis decks. The initial diagnosis pointed to operational inefficiency: 2,000+ hours of program data scattered across fragmented systems slowing alumni tracking, outreach, and fundraising coordination.

Intervention 1: I scoped and co-developed a unified data pipeline prototype to consolidate records and streamline operations.

Result: Minimal impact. The tool solved a symptom, not the root cause.

Lesson: Efficiency optimization can't fix a positioning problem. We were building the wrong thing.

Inflection Point: Reframing Through Field Immersion

After sustained internal debate and team fragmentation, attending Amber Waves' Manhattan fundraising event with 50+ donors provided critical perspective. The gap between founder passion, donor enthusiasm, and public awareness became visceral. This wasn't a data management problem—it was a go-to-market problem. The organization had product-market fit with existing users but couldn't scale acquisition because target audiences didn't understand the product.

We reset through a collaborative team-building workshop (building a 3-foot pyramid under time constraints), which clarified roles and rebuilt momentum. Sometimes the most important design work happens away from the computer.

Deep Dive: Perception Mapping & Audience Segmentation

I facilitated a 4-hour synthesis session mining 100+ pages of interview transcripts, identifying the core barrier: brand perception misalignment across multiple audience segments. Prospective apprentices saw exclusivity where accessibility existed; potential donors saw profit motive where nonprofit mission drove operations.

My contributions:

  • Audience perception mapping: Built Excel-based workflows consolidating outreach data, feedback loops, and conversion metrics across touchpoints
  • Persona pipeline development: Created three primary personas with corresponding empathy maps targeting apprentice candidates (ages 20-35), institutional donors, and community partners
  • Cross-functional coordination: Worked with engineering teammates coding brand strategy testing prototypes while I focused on qualitative frameworks

This phase taught me that perception is infrastructure. In social enterprises, misalignment between brand positioning and operational reality creates friction that compounds at scale—slowing talent pipelines, fundraising cycles, and community integration.

Validation & Strategic Pivot

Our low-fidelity prototype testing validated the reframe: repositioning Amber Waves as an accessible, mission-driven training program (rather than exclusive farm experience) generated 40% higher click-through rates from target demographics on demo day.

The Framework: Dual-Audience Positioning Strategy

Strategic Challenge

Scaling required simultaneous optimization across two distinct funnels:

  1. Apprentice acquisition (20-35 year-olds seeking career transitions into sustainable agriculture)
  2. Donor cultivation (100+ annual contributors funding program operations)

Traditional single-audience branding would sub-optimize one funnel to serve the other. We needed architecture that served both without dilution.

Solution Design

I designed and presented a dual-track positioning framework that maintained brand coherence while customizing messaging, touchpoints, and conversion pathways for each audience:

Track 1: Apprentice Pipeline

  • Repositioned program as "career accelerator in regenerative agriculture" rather than "farm internship"
  • Emphasized skills acquisition, alumni network, and career trajectory outcomes
  • Created social proof architecture highlighting diversity of apprentice backgrounds

Track 2: Donor Engagement

  • Quantified program impact through farmer shortage statistics and apprentice placement rates
  • Positioned donations as "infrastructure investment in food system resilience"
  • Built narrative connecting sustainable agriculture training to public health, economic development, and climate outcomes

Implementation Assets: Delivered 5+ strategy decks with phased rollout timelines, messaging guidelines, and channel-specific tactics. Designed implementation framework to fit within Amber Waves' bandwidth constraints—critical for resource-limited nonprofits.

Client Response

"Give us an immediate to-do list. We want to give our team no excuse not to implement this in the next cycle." — Amber Waves Founders

The clarity and actionability of the framework enabled immediate adoption, with results pending their next 8-month program cycle.

Reflection: Designing for Systems Change

This project crystallized three principles for high-impact consulting:

1. Problem Definition is Iterative
Our initial hypothesis (operational efficiency) was wrong. Rigorous problem reframing—anchored in field observation and cross-stakeholder synthesis—was the unlock.

2. Perception is Product
For mission-driven organizations, brand positioning isn't marketing—it's core infrastructure that enables or constrains growth. Misalignment creates market friction that no operational optimization can solve.

3. Scalability Requires Dual Optimization
Social enterprises must simultaneously optimize impact delivery (mission) and resource acquisition (capital/talent). Solutions that sub-optimize either dimension don't scale.

The experience shaped my approach to strategic design: start with stakeholder immersion, ruthlessly reframe problems, and design solutions that account for organizational constraints. In sectors where mission and market must coexist—healthcare, education, food systems—this mindset separates projects that ship from projects that matter.
