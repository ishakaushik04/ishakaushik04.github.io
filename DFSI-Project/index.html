Perception as the Scaling Constraint in a Sustainable-Farming Apprenticeship

Isha Kaushik

Prototype Design Research Lead · team of five across engineering, social work, education and medicine

Columbia Entrepreneurship Design Studio × SIPA · January to May 2025 · Client: Amber Waves

In short

A farmer-training nonprofit thought it had an operations problem. Over 40 interviews showed it had a perception problem. Reframing its brand drew 40% more click-through engagement from the target audience in early prototypes.

AbstractA women-led nonprofit that trains sustainable farmers asked for help scaling. The first hypothesis was operational: fragmented training data. A data pipeline was scoped, built and tested, and it had little effect. Over 40 stakeholder interviews then showed that operations were sound and the program was perceived as expensive and exclusive. The task was reframed as brand perception, three audience segments were mapped, and low-fidelity prototypes were tested against existing materials. At demo day the prototypes drew 40% more click-through engagement from the target demographic. The strategy was delivered as three prioritized decks and is rolling out in the 2025 program cycle. Outcome data are pending.

Background

The average U.S. farmer is 58, and 6 in 100 are under 35 (Fig. 1). The client trains future farmers in soil health, crop planning and food systems through an 8-month apprenticeship with 2,000 h of hands-on training and 20+ apprentices per year.

Problem

The stated brief was scale. Over 40 interviews (founders, apprentices, alumni, farm crew, community members, donors; 50+ pages of notes) placed the constraint in perception, not operations (Table 1).

Iteration

Results

Low-fidelity prototypes were compared with existing brand materials, with the target audience, at demo day. Prototypes produced 40% higher click-through engagement (Fig. 2).

Strategy

The core identity stays constant: a nonprofit training ground for sustainable agriculture. Messaging splits by audience (Table 3). A small team cannot run a long brand book, so the strategy was delivered as three decks: next steps at 30 days; a phased rollout across the 8-month cycle; and an effort-versus-impact ranking of tactics for limited time and budget.

Contributions

LedSynthesis of 40+ interviews into thematic decks; personas and empathy maps for apprentices, donors and potential supporters; the testing framework (what to test, how to measure, what counts as success).

SharedThe pivot from operations to perception; messaging, visual identity and outreach for two audiences; the three strategy decks. Engineers built the digital assets.

Discussion

The first hypothesis was rejected once stakeholder evidence was synthesized, so diagnosis determined the design. The strategy was scoped to what the client can run: ranked, time-boxed tactics.

Limitations

The engagement index comes from low-fidelity prototypes at demo day. Sample size and significance testing are not reported here. Outcomes (apprenticeship applications, donor engagement, community awareness) will be available after the 2025 cycle.
