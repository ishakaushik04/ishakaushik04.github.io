The stakes

Who will grow our food?

The average U.S. farmer is 58. The people who know how to grow food are aging out faster than new ones arrive. That's a public health problem as much as an agricultural one.

Out of every 100 U.S. farmers, 6 are under 35.

The client

A women-led nonprofit that trains future sustainable farmers through hands-on work in soil health, crop planning and food systems.

8 moapprenticeship
2,000 hof hands-on training
20+apprentices a year

The real problem

Perception, not operations.

The brief was “help us scale.” After 40+ interviews with founders, apprentices, alumni, farm crew, community members and donors, the picture changed: the program and the operations were solid. The bottleneck was how people saw it.

The journey

We solved the wrong problem first.

Explore→Reframe→Generate→Prototype→Pitch

  1. Wrong solution

    Hypothesis: 2,000 hours of apprenticeship data scattered across files was blocking scale. We scoped and built a unified data pipeline and tested it. It barely moved the needle. Data management was a symptom.

  2. Stuck for three weeks, then a room full of donors

    The team debated operations vs marketing vs different clients. Then we attended the Manhattan fundraiser: 50+ donors with real conviction for the mission. We left aligned to start over and do it right.

  3. Reframe

    A four-hour session through every page of notes made the pattern obvious: a perception disconnect. I led the mapping, with personas and empathy maps for three segments. They became the design criteria for every rebrand decision.

  4. Build and test

    Engineers prototyped website mockups, social content and donor communications. I designed the test: which engagement metrics would show perception had shifted.

The strategy

One identity, two messages.

The core stays constant: a nonprofit training ground for sustainable agriculture. The tactics split by audience.

AudienceWhat moves themWhat we lead with
Apprentices (20–35)Career change, hands-on learning, no college debt. Held back by cost and unclear outcomesAffordability and financial support, alumni stories, pathways to farm ownership
Donors (100+ give yearly)Environmental sustainability, education access, transparencyImpact numbers: apprentices trained, alumni staying in agriculture, community reach

Built to be used

  • Personas and empathy maps for apprentices, donors and potential supporters
  • The testing framework: what to test, how to measure, what counts as success

What we did as a team

  • Pivoted from operations to perception
  • Refined messaging, visual identity and outreach for two audiences
  • Wrote the three strategy decks. Engineers built the digital assets

What I took from it

The first hypothesis is rarely the right one.

The most important design skill is diagnosing the problem, and a strategy only counts if the client can and will run it. Outcome data will come once the 2025 cycle completes: apprentice applications, donor engagement and community awareness.
